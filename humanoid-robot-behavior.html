Humanoid robot behavior

A robot behavior is more useful when its task definition, version, success criteria and evaluation configuration are explicit and reproducible.

What is humanoid robot behavior?

Humanoid robot behavior describes how a humanoid system should act to accomplish a task: the goal, sequence of actions, constraints, observations, policies and conditions that define success. In research and engineering workflows, turning those ideas into structured specifications makes experiments easier to compare.

From behavior to measured evidence

A practical workflow separates the behavior contract from the execution engine. A team can define a behavior, assign a version, select policies and seeds, run a physics-based simulation, and retain the resulting metrics with the configuration that produced them.

Why version behavior definitions?

Behavior definitions evolve. Explicit versions make it possible to distinguish a change in the task specification from a change in the policy or simulation configuration. That distinction matters when investigating regressions and comparing experiments.
