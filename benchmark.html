METHODOLOGY

How scoring works

A benchmark is a controlled experiment, not a leaderboard shortcut. The same behavior, seeds and engine configuration run for every policy you compare.

What is measured

Each run records step completion — perceive the target, reach and grasp, transport, release and verify placement — alongside the overall outcome. A run passes when every required step completes within the stated conditions; a failed step is reported with the step name, so you can see whether a policy fails at perception, grasping or placement.

How to read a result

Read the outcome together with the behavior version, engine version, seeds, policy identifier and duration. A single run proves little: repeat with several seeds and compare the spread, not only the best result. When the evaluation setup stays stable, differences between runs can be attributed to the policy rather than to a changed task or configuration.

What a result does not prove

A MuJoCo result is evidence under stated simulated conditions. It does not prove hardware readiness. Hardware deployment requires separate validation, safety analysis and testing. Simulation-harness results are kept separate from measured simulator results and are labeled as measured=false.

BENCHMARK FAQ

Robot behavior benchmarking: common questions

What does a robot behavior benchmark measure?

A benchmark runs a defined behavior under stated conditions and reports task-relevant outcomes. Interpret results alongside the task version, engine, seeds, policy and measurement configuration.

How does HumanoidBehavior support reproducible evaluations?

The workflow keeps the behavior version, selected engine, seeds, policies and experiment results together so a team can inspect the configuration behind a run.

Can I run a benchmark without an account?

No. You need to sign in or create a free workspace before starting a measured benchmark. The free workspace currently includes 25 benchmark runs per month.

Does a MuJoCo benchmark prove a robot is ready for hardware?

No. A simulation result is evidence under the stated simulated conditions, not proof of real-world hardware readiness. Hardware deployment requires separate validation, safety analysis and testing.
